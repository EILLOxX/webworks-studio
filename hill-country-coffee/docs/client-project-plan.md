# 3. Target Users & User Needs

## Maya — Mobile Regular

**Context:**  
Maya lives in the area and stops for coffee several times each month. She usually visits the website from her phone while she is already out.

**Primary goal:**
Find the closest cafe information

**Important information/tasks:**
she Scans rather than reads long blocks of text, Often visits with a specific task in mind. she also check whether a familiar drink or food option is available.

**Likely friction:**
menu information is difficult to scan on a small screen, navigation labels are vague, and pages load with large amounts of promotional material.

**Design/accessibility implication:**
touch-friendly controls, readable text, and predictable navigations

## Daniel — Hill Country Visitor

**Context:**
Daniel is visiting Central Texas and searches online for local coffee shops while planning a day trip. He has never visited Hill Country Coffee before.

**Primary goal:**
understand what kind of place Hill Country Coffee is and decide whether it fits his plans

**Important information/tasks:**
he uses both phone and laptop while planning, Looks for recognizable information patterns such as Menu, Locations, About, and Hours. and Uses photographs and concise descriptive copy to evaluate unfamiliar places.

**Likely friction:**
a site assumes he already knows the business, location information lacks context, navigation uses clever but unclear labels, and important information appears only inside images.

**Design/accessibility implication:**
To him Content should make sense regardless of entry page, and important information should be a real text inside of hidden in images.

## Renee — Deliberate Planner

**Context:**
Renee likes meeting friends at local cafés but prefers to review options before choosing a location. She may spend more time reading than Maya or Daniel.

**Primary goal:**
review menu categories

**Important information/tasks:**
She would like to be able to identify food and beverage choices and to understand the atmosphere and values of the business and be able to share useful information with a friend and plan a visit without needing to call the café for basic information

**Likely friction:**
links such as “click here” provide little context, layouts break when text is enlarged, text is small or low contrast, menus depend on tiny PDF documents or images

**Design/accessibility implication:**
Readable contrast, flexible layouts, meaningful headings, descriptive links, and text that remains usable when enlarged are particularly important.
